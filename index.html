How Ernest Lab works

1 · Quick start

  • Drop your photos anywhere on the window, or use Add images. JPEG, PNG, WebP and camera RAW (DNG, CR2/CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, RW2, ORF, PEF and more).
  • Development prepares the photo first: format / crop, the profile (tone curve), then shadows, midtones and highlights.
  • Film stock picks the look; Look strength blends it in.
  • Fine-tune any section, then Export this still or Export all.

RAW files

  • RAW files are developed in your browser with LibRaw: camera white balance, full sensor range, straight into Ernest Lab's linear working space, so highlights roll off like film instead of clipping like a JPEG.
  • Base exposure is matched to the camera's own preview in the file, so a RAW opens as bright as the camera saw it (shown next to the file name). Adjust with Exposure, or per image with Development.
  • Profile → Camera-like (the default for RAW) gives it the contrast of the camera's own JPEG, measured from the preview inside the file, while keeping the RAW's extra highlight detail. Flat leaves the linear data untouched, for building contrast yourself.
  • Opening a RAW takes a few seconds (longer on phones). Only the still you're looking at is held in memory; switching back to a RAW develops it again.
  • The decoder (about 1.5 MB) loads the first time you open a RAW, then also works offline.

Video

  • Add MP4, MOV or WebM clips like stills (CG renders and AI video are perfect for this). Everything works the same: Development, looks, black & white, halation, lens and grain.
  • Play and scrub with the bar under the picture (K plays / pauses). Grain is different on every frame, like real film.
  • Export video (MP4) renders every frame at full resolution and encodes H.264 with the original sound. It takes a while (it isn't real time); the clip never leaves your device. Export all bundles stills only.
  • The clip has to play in this browser: H.264 MP4 works everywhere; HEVC / ProRes depend on the browser. HDR clips are shown as standard video for now.

Black & white

  • Film is one layer of silver, and what it "sees" decides which colours become which greys. Pick a Film: Panchromatic (modern Tri-X / HP5: the whole spectrum, a little more blue than your eye), Orthochromatic (red-blind: dark reds and lips, pale skies, silent-film faces), Blue-sensitive (1800s plates), Extended red, Infrared-like (glowing foliage, black skies) or Digital luminance (a computer's desaturate, matched to the eye).
  • A Lens filter lightens its own colour and darkens the opposite one: yellow for a slightly darker sky, orange and red for dramatic skies and smooth skin, green for brighter foliage, blue for haze. On a real camera each filter costs light; Ernest Lab compensates, so mid grey stays put.
  • The Red / Green / Blue counts show the mix the film and filter add up to; move them for your own. Look at the R G B channels in the header to predict what a filter will do.
  • Silver has no colour, so in black & white the halation glow, lens fringes and grain are grey too. Toning (selenium, sepia) comes from Shadow / Highlight tint in Colour; see the B&W looks.

Profiles & the tone curve

  • The curve in Development works in stops around mid grey (0 = mid grey, +1 = twice as bright), over the photo's histogram. Drag a point; click or tap the curve to add one; double-click or double-tap a point to remove it.
  • It changes brightness only (luminance), so colours keep their hue, and it runs before Shadows / Midtones / Highlights and before the film stock.
  • Save profile keeps a curve in this browser under My profiles; Export / Load… move it between devices as .json (you can also drop it on the image). Each still keeps its own profile; Apply to all stills copies it.
  • JPEGs already carry the camera's curve, so their default is As shot (no change).

2 · Batch: one look, individual development

  • The film look (stock, grade, halation, lens, grain) applies to every still in the strip, like shooting one roll of film.
  • Development is per image. Each still keeps its own format, framing and tones, so you can rescue a dark shot without touching the others.
  • Apply to all stills copies the current image's development to the whole strip.
  • Export all renders every still at full resolution into one zip, with the look's look.json.

3 · Compare

  • Click the image to flip original / film; hold Space to peek at the original.
  • Before / after splits the view; drag on the image to move the split.
  • R G B L show one channel in greyscale (L = luminance); press it again for colour.
  • The switch on each section turns it off, to see exactly what it does. Off is also off in exports.
  • Every section has a ?: what it is on real film, what you'll see, and one thing to try.
  • Double-click a slider's value to reset it. Drag the value left / right to change it (Shift for fine); Exposure, the Development tones and a few others go past the slider's end that way (the number turns orange).
  • Zoom: mouse wheel or trackpad pinch zooms around the cursor, drag to pan, double-click the photo for 100% (again for Fit). Keys 0 Fit, 1 100%, + / −. In before / after, pan with the middle button or Alt-drag.
  • On a phone: tap the photo to flip, touch and hold to peek at the original, pinch to zoom in and check the grain, tap a value to reset it. The tabs at the bottom hold the controls; ⋯ has channels, zoom and Clear session.

4 · Looks

  • Save look keeps it in this browser under My looks.
  • To move looks between devices: Settings JSON, Load look…, or drop a .json on the image.
  • Grain stock changes only the grain, independent of the colour look. Emulsion layers fine-tune the red, green and blue grain.

5 · Output

  • JPEG or PNG at full resolution, at the cropped size when a format is set. The hint under Format shows which platforms won't crop it further.
  • Share… and Copy image appear where your browser supports them (on phones, Share lists Instagram, WhatsApp…).
  • Export look as .cube takes the colour part of the look to Resolve or Nuke (in ACES, apply it after the output transform).

6 · Install as an app & privacy

Installed, Ernest Lab gets its own icon and window and works offline. It updates itself when a new version is out.

  • iPhone / iPad (Safari): tap Share (square with an arrow; on newer iOS it's behind … next to the address bar) → Add to Home Screen → Add. Not in the list? Scroll down → Edit Actions. In Chrome on iPhone it's also under Share.
  • Android (Chrome): ⋮ menu → Install app (or Add to Home screen) → Install.
  • Computer (Chrome / Edge): the install icon at the right end of the address bar, or menu → Cast, save and share / Apps → Install.
  • Mac (Safari): File → Add to Dock.
  • Then open it from the new icon, not from the browser.
  • Everything runs on your device. Your photos are never uploaded.

Ernest Lab

Film looks for digital stills: development, film-stock colour, lens, halation and three-layer emulsion grain, processed in scene-linear light in the order a lens and a negative would.

Private by design. Everything runs in your browser with WebGL2. Your images never leave your device: Ernest Lab uploads nothing, has no account, no analytics and no tracking, and loads nothing from other sites. The page itself is hosted on GitHub Pages.

Film stock names describe the stocks that inspired each look. Ernest Lab is not affiliated with or endorsed by any film manufacturer; the looks are original approximations, not measured profiles.
